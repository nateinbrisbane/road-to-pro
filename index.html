In 2025 I raced HYROX Brisbane in the Open division and finished in 1:20:54. Now I want to race Pro.

This is a social experiment: can AI coach me from Open into the Pro category? Everything here is run by Grok Bot. It wrote my training plan, syncs my Garmin activities and recovery (sleep and HRV), captures my food intake every day, and updates this site. But the numbers are only half of it. After every session I also log how it felt, my mood, energy and headspace, because mental toughness and knowing myself are what most programmes never track. Every week Grok Bot adjusts the plan using both sides: what my body did and how my head handled it.

I'm not racing anyone else, I'm racing the old me, chasing continuous improvement with structure, rigour and progress I can measure, in body and mind.

The main race is HYROX Brisbane Pro on Fri 2 Apr 2027, where the goal is sub 1:10. Later in 2027 I'll race HYROX Sydney in Mixed Doubles.

Session review›

Test: sled push at Pro load (205 kg including the sled), 4×12.5 m. 18, 20, 20 and 22 seconds, so 80 seconds for the full 50 m. Only 4 seconds of fade across the four lengths.

Test: sled pull at Pro load (155 kg including the sled), 4×12.5 m. 28, 28, 32 and 37 seconds, so 125 seconds for 50 m. The pull faded by 9 seconds, more than twice the push, which makes it the main weakness to work on.

Training: 5 rounds of 25 m sled push, 25 m sled pull and a 200 m run. Round 1 took 5:20 including the hook change for the pull, with the run at about 4:55/km. Round 2 used outdoor laps instead of the treadmill and wasn't timed. Round 3 took 5:30 with the run at about 4:00/km. Rounds 4 and 5 were completed.

Finisher: sandbag lunges at 30 kg. Set 1 was strong, set 2 got harder in the second half, and sets 3 and 4 were combined into one 50 m piece with a few breaks.

Takeaway. The Pro sled weights went better than expected. A highlight was talking with an athlete two weeks out from Melbourne Pro. Learning from the big boys and training around a room full of people focused on HYROX was a great part of the morning. One tip from him: race surfaces can be slippery, so he trains the pull at Pro push weight. That's worth trying at the next retest. Another tip from him: on the sled push, take a short, deliberate stop halfway through each length rather than driving straight through. That saves the legs for the later stations. A third tip: do threshold runs at race pace, with the rest between reps set to match your Roxzone time. Also learn how long it takes you to recover when it gets hard. He knows he comes back within about 200 m after the Roxzone, so he keeps pushing through the hard patch instead of backing off.
