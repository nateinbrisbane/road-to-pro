HYROX Brisbane Pro · Men 45–49 · Fri 2 Apr 2027

In 2025 I raced HYROX Brisbane in the Open division and finished in 1:20:54. Now I want to race Pro.

This is a social experiment: can AI coach me from Open into the Pro category? Everything here is run by Grok Bot. It wrote my training plan, syncs my Garmin activities and recovery (sleep and HRV), captures my food intake every day, and updates this site. But the numbers are only half of it. After every session I also log how it felt, my mood, energy and headspace, because mental toughness and knowing myself are what most programmes never track. Every week Grok Bot adjusts the plan using both sides: what my body did and how my head handled it.

I'm not racing anyone else, I'm racing the old me, chasing continuous improvement with structure, rigour and progress I can measure, in body and mind.

The main race is HYROX Brisbane Pro on Fri 2 Apr 2027, where the goal is sub 1:10. Later in 2027 I'll race HYROX Sydney in Mixed Doubles.

Session review›

Chased the 4:00–4:05/km target up a +14.5 m climb on the first 1 km rep—holding 4:05–4:08/km for the first 600 m took 519–550 W of running power and burned a huge match before the set really started. Settled into a much cleaner rhythm on the flat for Reps 2 and 3 at 4:21/km (451 W, 183 spm) and 4:19/km (438 W, 182 spm), pushing ~42 W more power and a 7–10 s/km faster grade-adjusted pace than last week’s tempo.

With yesterday’s 100 kg deadlifts, 100 kg rope pulls and 300 m farmers carries still in the posterior chain, the legs hit a mechanical wall after 400 m uphill on Rep 4 (457 W) and 400 m into Rep 5 (4:24/km), so I called the last rep early and ran the cool-down home.

Takeaways for next week: cap uphill interval starts by power (~440–450 W) instead of forcing raw pace on a grade, target 4:15–4:20/km on flat reps while building speed after heavy pull days, and load extra carbs on Tuesday evening.

Fri30 Oct HIIT class 552 kcal ›
43 min duration552 kcal+18 min strength
Session review›

Part 1: pre-class strength (18 min). Dips with no added load: narrow grip 1×8 and 2×15, wide grip 1×8 and 1×15. Calf raises 5×12 at 85 kg. Cross-body hammer curls, one set of 12 at each of 10, 12.5, 15, 17.5 and 20 kg.

Part 2: gym HIIT class, top option at every station. Block 1 (14 min): 20 kettlebell high pulls (20 kg), 15 cal assault bike, 30 pop squats, 15 cal row, 30 dumbbell thrusters (2×10 kg), 15 cal SkiErg, 100 m ball carry, and 6×150 m run laps. Block 2 (19 min): 50 plate front squats (20 kg), 15 box jumps, 50 plate overhead presses (20 kg), 20 burpees, 50 reverse lunges with a 20 kg plate (25 per leg), 60 shoulder taps (30 per side) and 60 butterflies.

Part 3: SkiErg partner relay. My training partner and I alternated 30-second efforts for just over 5 minutes and covered 1,370 m between us.

Reflection 1: patience. Long sets at a heavy weight, like 50 reps, aren't hard. I know I can grind through them, and that's exactly why they get boring. Somewhere around rep 20-something I'm thinking, "Oh man, this is so boring." That's when you need the patience to finish the set, because it's usually the last few reps that make the difference.

Reflection 2: teamwork. In a two-person ski relay you have to back your teammate, pull your weight and do your best so you don't let the team down. I love the camaraderie of pushing each other through the hard parts, especially the last stint of the race. And when you win, it's an amazing feeling.
